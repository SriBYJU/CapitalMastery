# Capital Mastery — 15-Minute Employer Demo Playbook

## Demo goal

Do not try to show every feature. The 15-minute story should answer five employer questions:

1. **What problem does this solve that normal onboarding does not?**
2. **What does a learner actually do?**
3. **How do we know whether the work was good enough?**
4. **What can a manager do with the evidence?**
5. **Can the firm govern/customize the program without weakening the standard?**

Use synthetic/demo data unless the firm has explicitly authorized a real pilot environment.

---

## 0:00–1:30 — The problem: completion is not readiness

Open the employer landing page.

Explain:

- normal onboarding covers policies, systems, logistics and culture;
- Capital Mastery focuses on role-specific finance work before Day 1;
- the platform is free for employers and learners;
- public workforce research provides context for the onboarding problem but is **not** a promised Capital Mastery outcome.

**Line to land:**

> “The question we are trying to answer is not whether an analyst watched training. It is whether there is evidence they can perform the work at the standard you expect.”

Do not open with a credential page. Start with the employer problem.

---

## 1:30–3:00 — Choose the preparation level

Open a career page or Quick Assign and show the two-program architecture.

### Career Skills Program

Explain that it covers Foundations → Essentials → Applied Skills with guided/independent practice and a practical completion experience.

### Professional Readiness Program

Explain that it carries those first three milestones forward and adds Role Lab → revisions/evidence → Professional Readiness.

**Important:** show that the shorter route is not MCQ-only and that upgrading does not force learners to repeat completed verified milestones.

**Line to land:**

> “You choose the depth of preparation; the evidence model stays explicit about what the learner has and has not demonstrated.”

---

## 3:00–4:30 — Command Center: completion vs readiness

Open a synthetic Mixed Cohort.

Point out:

- learners / started / ready or complete;
- average readiness where applicable;
- evidence coverage;
- Manager Attention Queue;
- cohort/assignment status.

Click one attention signal and explain **why** it was surfaced—deadline, revision or evidence gap—not an arbitrary engagement score.

**Line to land:**

> “A high completion rate can still hide a model-review problem. That is why these measures are separated.”

---

## 4:30–6:00 — Curriculum + Firm Layer: standardization without rigidity

Open the curriculum review room.

Show:

1. protected **Capital Mastery Standard**;
2. role teaching and practice sequence;
3. Firm Layer additions;
4. edit / reorder;
5. version history;
6. hide / archive / restore;
7. protected-standard denial if a required component is targeted.

Do **not** spend the demo typing a long content item. One concise firm-specific example is enough.

**Line to land:**

> “The firm can make the program sound and feel like its environment without quietly weakening what the credential means.”

---

## 6:00–9:30 — Learner work: prove this is not another quiz product

This is the most important portion of the demo.

Open a role-native toolkit / Applied Skills surface and then the Role Lab.

For Investment Banking, for example, show some combination of:

- source/deal material;
- spreadsheet-style valuation/model work;
- model checks;
- changing transaction information;
- analyst recommendation / manager handoff.

For another role, use its native work product: LBO/IC, cap table/memo, forecast/variance, cash schedule, execution decision, risk scenario, client proposal, real-estate underwriting, etc.

Then show the Role Lab revision loop:

1. first submission;
2. specific weakness or failed requirement;
3. feedback / required change;
4. revised work;
5. updated evidence.

**Line to land:**

> “We do not just ask what the learner knows. We make them use the skill, respond to changed information, and leave an evidence trail when the first version is not good enough.”

Avoid spending these minutes scrolling through ordinary multiple-choice items; the professional work is the differentiator.

---

## 9:30–12:00 — Readiness evidence: turn learner work into manager action

Open the employer readiness report and drill into one synthetic learner.

Show:

- current stage;
- readiness vs completion;
- evidence coverage;
- competency scores / critical floors;
- Role Lab score;
- revision history;
- final result;
- evidence/work-product detail where available;
- manager review note.

Then return to the cohort view and show the lowest recurring competency.

Export CSV or evidence JSON only if time allows; explain that protected assessment keys are not included and CSV cells are hardened against spreadsheet-formula injection.

**Line to land:**

> “The score matters less than whether a manager can trace the signal back to the work and decide what to coach.”

---

## 12:00–13:15 — Credentials: show what was actually demonstrated

Show the five Standard 2.0 career milestones:

1. Foundations
2. Essentials
3. Applied Skills
4. Role Lab
5. Professional Readiness

Explain:

- Career Skills uses the first three verified milestones plus its program-completion document;
- Professional Readiness uses all five;
- the Career Skills completion certificate is not a hidden sixth Standard 2.0 credential;
- public verification is privacy-conscious and excludes private account identifiers and assessment answers;
- Academy credentials are a separate credential family.

Open one public verification example if available.

---

## 13:15–14:15 — Governance and trust

Open Team & Roles / governance visual and explain the five roles:

- Owner
- Training Admin
- Content Manager
- Manager
- Viewer

Show Audit Log or Firm Layer history and explain that role checks are enforced by the Worker, not merely hidden buttons.

Briefly show the Trust Center and Admin Demo/Test Lab.

Avoid claiming SOC 2, ISO, regulatory approval or other third-party certification that Capital Mastery has not documented.

---

## 14:15–15:00 — Propose a measurable pilot

Offer a narrow first pilot:

- one finance role;
- one cohort;
- Career Skills or Professional Readiness;
- one agreed deadline;
- manager-defined success metrics before launch.

Suggested evidence to compare:

- baseline diagnostic;
- work-product / Role Lab performance;
- revision patterns;
- competency gaps;
- manager coaching priorities;
- post-start time to first independently acceptable work product, if the firm can measure it consistently.

**Closing line:**

> “The pilot does not need to assume Capital Mastery works. Give us one cohort, define what useful readiness evidence would look like before we start, and judge the platform on whether the work and manager signals are actually useful.”

---

## If the firm asks deeper questions

### “Can we customize it?”
Yes—through the Firm Layer, while the protected standardized credential requirements remain separate.

### “Can managers see everything?”
No. Employer access is role-scoped. Content administration and learner-performance access are deliberately separated.

### “Is this replacing our training?”
No. It can complement HR onboarding, firm-specific training and supervision by handling standardized role preparation/evidence.

### “What does ‘ready’ guarantee?”
Nothing about hiring or future job performance. Readiness is a defined Capital Mastery evidence state tied to the program requirements and should be interpreted alongside employer judgment.

### “Why is it free?”
Capital Mastery is currently positioned as free workforce-readiness infrastructure for learners and employers; there is no subscription/seat/trial gate in the product.

### “Can we test it without real learners?”
Yes—the administrator Demo/Test Lab uses synthetic data for cohort, readiness, revision and permission scenarios.
