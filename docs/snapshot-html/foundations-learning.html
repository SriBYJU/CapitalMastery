How Investment Banking actually works

PURPOSE

Advise corporate and sponsor clients on major strategic and financing transactions.

CLIENT / CAPITAL

Corporations, financial sponsors, boards and other institutional clients.

ECONOMICS

Advisory and underwriting fees tied to transactions and capital raising.

A realistic day

  1. 01

    Start by reviewing new information relevant to investment banking analyst work and prioritizing the highest-impact assignment.

  2. 02

    Spend a substantial block of time producing or reviewing comparable-company analysis and related analysis.

  3. 03

    Coordinate with teammates and stakeholders while checking assumptions, sources and presentation quality.

  4. 04

    Finish by updating the core work product, documenting open questions and preparing the next decision or review.

Language of the desk

Tap each term. Capital Mastery teaches the plain-English meaning, professional use and common mistake.

EBITDAOpen explanation
SIMPLE

Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; a commonly used operating-profit measure.

AT WORK

Used in valuation multiples, credit analysis and deal screening.

COMMON MISTAKE

Treating EBITDA as cash flow or ignoring company-specific adjustments.

Enterprise ValueOpen explanation
SIMPLE

The value of a company’s operating business attributable to all capital providers.

AT WORK

Often paired with operating metrics such as revenue or EBITDA.

COMMON MISTAKE

Confusing enterprise value with equity value or forgetting net debt and other claims.

Equity ValueOpen explanation
SIMPLE

The value attributable to common shareholders.

AT WORK

Used in per-share valuation, market capitalization and transaction analysis.

COMMON MISTAKE

Using equity value with pre-interest metrics such as EBITDA.

DCFOpen explanation
SIMPLE

A valuation method that estimates value from the present value of expected future free cash flows.

AT WORK

Used when analysts can form a reasoned view of long-term cash generation.

COMMON MISTAKE

Treating a DCF as precise rather than assumption-sensitive.

WACCOpen explanation
SIMPLE

A blended required return for debt and equity capital, weighted by their contribution to capital structure.

AT WORK

Often used as the discount rate for unlevered free cash flow in a DCF.

COMMON MISTAKE

Using inconsistent capital structure, market values or risk assumptions.

Terminal ValueOpen explanation
SIMPLE

The estimated value of cash flows beyond an explicit forecast period.

AT WORK

Usually a large component of enterprise value in a DCF.

COMMON MISTAKE

Using unrealistic perpetual growth or exit multiple assumptions.

Comparable CompaniesOpen explanation
SIMPLE

A relative valuation method using trading multiples of similar public companies.

AT WORK

Provides a market-based valuation reference range.

COMMON MISTAKE

Choosing peers only because they are in the same broad sector.

Precedent TransactionsOpen explanation
SIMPLE

A valuation method using multiples paid in relevant historical M&A transactions.

AT WORK

Helps assess control valuations and transaction premiums.

COMMON MISTAKE

Ignoring differences in timing, market conditions, synergies or deal structure.

SynergiesOpen explanation
SIMPLE

Economic benefits expected from combining businesses, such as cost savings or revenue opportunities.

AT WORK

Can influence strategic value and the price a buyer is willing to pay.

COMMON MISTAKE

Counting vague or unachievable synergies as certain.

CIMOpen explanation
SIMPLE

Confidential Information Memorandum; a detailed marketing document used in many company sale processes.

AT WORK

Buyers use it to understand the business before deeper diligence.

COMMON MISTAKE

Treating management projections as independently verified facts.

LOIOpen explanation
SIMPLE

Letter of Intent; a document outlining major proposed transaction terms, often mostly non-binding.

AT WORK

Helps move a transaction toward exclusivity and confirmatory diligence.

COMMON MISTAKE

Assuming every provision is binding or the deal is certain to close.

Accretion / DilutionOpen explanation
SIMPLE

Analysis of whether a transaction increases or decreases a buyer’s earnings per share.

AT WORK

Common in public-company M&A analysis.

COMMON MISTAKE

Assuming accretive always means strategically attractive.
